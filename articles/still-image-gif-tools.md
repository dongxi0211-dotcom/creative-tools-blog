# 3 GIF Makers for Animating a Still Image, Ranked by How Much You Need to Draw

Take a photo of a white house by the sea. You want the water to shimmer, but the blue door should stay exactly where it is. There are three ways to make that loop: ask a tool to invent the movement, assemble photos that already capture it, or draw the changes yourself. The ranking below starts with the least drawing required for someone who has just one picture.

*Disclosure: This guide was contributed by the team behind AI Image to Image. Its coastal example comes from the project's saved output; the Ezgif and GIMP entries use their official documentation.*

## 1. AI Image to Image — when you have one picture and no motion frames

The [AI Image to Image GIF maker](https://ai-image-to-image.io/image-to-gif?utm_source=github.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=still_image_gif_tools&utm_content=description_link) takes a still image and a description of what should move. In the coastal example, the prompt asks for the sea to shimmer and the flowers to sway while the house and path stay still. Six generated poses play forward and back to make a two-second loop.

Watch the doorframe on the second viewing. A straight edge makes it easier to spot unintended movement than a patch of rippling water does. If the architecture must stay pixel-perfect, use the layer approach in entry three.

## 2. Ezgif — when your camera already caught the movement

Suppose you took a burst of the same scene. The ripples you need are already in those photos. [Ezgif's GIF maker](https://ezgif.com/maker) lets you put them in order and set how long each frame stays on screen. With a burst on hand, this would be my first pick: you can keep the actual water, light and reflections.

Try just three shots first. Line up the doorframe as you flip through them. If it jumps, align the photos before adding the rest. Changing the frame delay can slow a wave, but it won't steady a handheld camera.

## 3. GIMP — when only the pixels you choose may change

For a tiny illustrated lighthouse, you might want one amber window to blink while every line of the drawing stays fixed. Follow [GIMP's animation tutorial](https://www.gimp.org/tutorials/Simple_Animations/) to build the GIF from layers. Start with two copies of the drawing, paint the lit window on one, and preview the blink before exporting.

Two frames can be enough here. Leave the dark frame on screen longer than the lit one and the light reads as a signal; give them equal time and it feels more like a flashing button. That timing decision matters more to this particular drawing than adding a dozen new frames.
